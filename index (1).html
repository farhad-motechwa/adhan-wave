      Time per second

      Each bright line marks where the sun is 18° below the horizon (Fajr, astronomical twilight) or at sunset (Maghrib, no safety margin added). It is a circle around the point where the sun is overhead, so it curves, and at high latitudes it bends sharply. A hollow dot means the sun never reaches that angle in that city on the chosen date.

      Times are calculated from the sun's position for each city, not copied from a mosque timetable. We checked the method on 1 Nov 2026 against published timetables for ten cities (Makkah, Istanbul, Kuala Lumpur, Jakarta, Singapore, London, New York, Perth, Sydney, Cairo): Maghrib was within 0 to 3 minutes in nine of them (Istanbul was 8 early, from a margin the Diyanet adds). Fajr was within about 2 minutes where the authority's own angle was selected; London Central Mosque's Fajr is about 15 minutes later than at 18°. Some authorities use other angles or margins, and Jaʿfari timetables place Maghrib later. For worship, follow your local timetable and scholars.
